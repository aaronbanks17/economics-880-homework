\section*{Question 1}

The moment vector $m_3(x_t)$ contains the quantities used to
construct the mean, variance, and first-order autocovariance of
the time series $x \equiv \{x_t\}$. We have
\[
    m_3(x_t)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        x_t\\
        (x_t-\bar{x})^2\\
        (x_t-\bar{x})(x_{t-1}-\bar{x})
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

We will consider the moments of the stationary distribution of the
series since we are asked to compute the asymptotic moments.
We have
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{E}[x_t]
    &= \rho_0 \mathbb{E}[x_{t-1}]
    + \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t]\\
    &= \rho_0 \mathbb{E}[x_{t-1}].
\end{align*}
Under stationarity, $\mathbb{E}[x_t]=\mu$ for all $t$, hence
\[
    \mu = \rho_0\mu
    \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad
    \mu(1-\rho_0)=0
    \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad
    \mu=0,
\]
since $\rho_0=0.5$.

For the asymptotic variance, we have
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{E}[x_t^2]
    &= \rho_0^2\mathbb{E}[x_{t-1}^2]
    + 2\rho_0\mathbb{E}[x_{t-1}\varepsilon_t]
    + \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t^2]\\
    &= \rho_0^2\mathbb{E}[x_{t-1}^2]+\sigma_0^2,
\end{align*}
since $\mathbb{E}[x_{t-1}\varepsilon_t]=0$.
Under stationarity,
$\mathbb{E}[x_{t-1}^2]=\mathbb{E}[x_t^2]=v$, so
\[
    v=\rho_0^2v+\sigma_0^2
    \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad
    v=\frac{\sigma_0^2}{1-\rho_0^2}.
\]

For the first-order autocovariance, we have
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{E}[x_tx_{t-1}]
    &= \mathbb{E}[
        (\rho_0x_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t)x_{t-1}
    ]\\
    &= \rho_0\mathbb{E}[x_{t-1}^2]
    + \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_tx_{t-1}]\\
    &= \rho_0\mathbb{E}[x_{t-1}^2]\\
    &= \frac{\rho_0\sigma_0^2}{1-\rho_0^2},
\end{align*}
where the third equality follows from the independence of
$\varepsilon_t$ and $x_{t-1}$, and the fourth from stationarity.

Thus, for the parameter vector
$b=(\rho,\sigma^2)^{\mathsf T}$, the population moment vector is
\[
    \mu_3(b)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0\\[6pt]
        \displaystyle\frac{\sigma^2}{1-\rho^2}\\[10pt]
        \displaystyle\frac{\rho\sigma^2}{1-\rho^2}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

Evaluating at the true parameters,
$b_0=(\rho_0,\sigma_0^2)^{\mathsf T}$, gives
\[
    \mu_3(b_0)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0\\[6pt]
        \displaystyle\frac{\sigma_0^2}{1-\rho_0^2}\\[10pt]
        \displaystyle\frac{\rho_0\sigma_0^2}{1-\rho_0^2}
    \end{bmatrix}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0\\
        4/3\\
        2/3
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

We next compute the Jacobian of the population moment conditions.
Recall that
\[
    g(b)=\mu_3(b_0)-\mu_3(b),
\]
so
\[
    \nabla_b g(b)
    =
    -\frac{\partial\mu_3(b)}{\partial b^{\mathsf T}}.
\]

Differentiating with respect to $\rho$ and $\sigma^2$, we obtain
\[
    \nabla_b g(b)
    =
    -
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & 0\\[6pt]
        \displaystyle\frac{2\rho\sigma^2}{(1-\rho^2)^2}
        &
        \displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\rho^2}\\[10pt]
        \displaystyle\frac{\sigma^2(1+\rho^2)}{(1-\rho^2)^2}
        &
        \displaystyle\frac{\rho}{1-\rho^2}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

Evaluating at $b_0$, we have
\[
    G \equiv \nabla_b g(b_0)
    =
    -
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & 0\\
        16/9 & 4/3\\
        20/9 & 2/3
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The Jacobian has full column rank since
\[
    \det
    \begin{bmatrix}
        16/9 & 4/3\\
        20/9 & 2/3
    \end{bmatrix}
    =
    -\frac{16}{9}\neq 0.
\]
Thus,
\[
    \operatorname{rank}(G)=2,
\]
and the two structural parameters are locally identified.

We next compute the AGS sensitivity matrix, defined as
\[
    \Lambda
    =
    -(G^{\mathsf T}WG)^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}W.
\]

Using $W=I_3$, we have
\[
    \Lambda
    =
    -(G^{\mathsf T}G)^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Substituting the Jacobian and simplifying gives
\[
    \Lambda
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & -3/8 & 3/4\\
        0 & 5/4 & -1
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The first column of $\Lambda$ is zero, indicating that the
sample mean does not affect either parameter estimate when
$W=I_3$. This follows from the fact that the population mean
is zero regardless of the structural parameters.

The variance and first-order autocovariance are informative
for both parameters. In particular, the first row of $\Lambda$
indicates that an increase in the empirical variance decreases
the estimate of $\rho$, holding the autocovariance fixed,
while an increase in the empirical autocovariance increases
the estimate of $\rho$, holding the variance fixed.

Similarly, the second row indicates that an increase in
the empirical variance increases the estimate of $\sigma^2$,
holding the autocovariance fixed, while an increase in the
empirical autocovariance decreases the estimate of $\sigma^2$,
holding the variance fixed.

Thus, the mean provides no identifying information, while
the variance and autocovariance jointly identify the two
structural parameters.

\section*{Questions 4--6}

In the numerical implementation, we
parameterize the model by
\[
    b = (\rho,\sigma)^{\mathsf T},
\]
where $\sigma$ denotes the standard deviation of the innovations,
rather than their variance as in Question 1.

For a given set of moments, the SMM objective is
\[
    J_{TH}(b)
    =
    g_{TH}(b)^{\mathsf T}Wg_{TH}(b),
\]
where
\[
    g_{TH}(b)
    =
    M_T(x)-M_{TH}(y(b)).
\]

We first obtain $\widehat b_1$ using $W_1=I$. We then construct
the Newey--West covariance estimator with four lags,
\[
    \widehat S_{TH}
    =
    \left(1+\frac{1}{H}\right)
    \widehat S_{y,TH},
\]
and reestimate the parameters using
\[
    W_2=\widehat S_{TH}^{-1}.
\]
The resulting estimator is denoted $\widehat b_2$.

For inference, we approximate the Jacobian
$G=\nabla_b g_{TH}(\widehat b_2)$ numerically and calculate
\[
    \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\widehat b_2)
    =
    \frac{1}{T}
    (G^{\mathsf T}W_2G)^{-1},
\]
along with the AGS sensitivity matrix
\[
    \Lambda
    =
    -(G^{\mathsf T}W_2G)^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}W_2.
\]

Because the weighting matrix already incorporates the
$(1+1/H)$ simulation adjustment, the J-test statistic is
\[
    J_{\mathrm{test}}
    =
    T J_{TH}(\widehat b_2).
\]

\section*{Question 4}

We first consider the just-identified specification matching
the mean and variance:
\[
    M_T(x)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \bar x\\
        \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(x)
    \end{bmatrix}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.217749\\
        1.533191
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

From Question 1, the corresponding population moments are
\[
    \mu_4(b)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0\\[4pt]
        \displaystyle\frac{\sigma^2}{1-\rho^2}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Their Jacobian at the true parameters, using the
parameterization $b=(\rho,\sigma)^{\mathsf T}$, is
\[
    G_4(b_0)
    =
    -
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & 0\\
        16/9 & 8/3
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The Jacobian has rank one. The mean provides no identifying
information, while the variance identifies only the
combination $\sigma^2/(1-\rho^2)$. Consequently,
$\rho$ and $\sigma$ are not separately identified.

\paragraph{(a) First-stage estimation.}

Minimizing the SMM objective with $W_1=I_2$ gives
\[
    \widehat b_1
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.998306\\
        0.208981
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    J_{TH}(\widehat b_1)=0.006651.
\]

Figure \ref{fig:smm_surfaces}(a) presents the objective
surface over the specified parameter grid. The estimated
parameters lie outside this grid, with the autoregressive
coefficient approaching the stationarity boundary.

This behavior is consistent with the identification problem:
different combinations of $\rho$ and $\sigma$ can generate
similar unconditional variances.

\paragraph{(b) Second-stage estimation.}

Using the Newey--West covariance estimator, we obtain
\[
    W_2
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.047149 & -0.000057\\
        -0.000057 & 0.029513
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The second-stage optimizer returns
\[
    \widehat b_2
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.998306\\
        0.208981
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    J_{TH}(\widehat b_2)=0.000313.
\]

However, the second-stage optimization did not satisfy its
convergence criterion.

\paragraph{(c) Standard errors and AGS statistics.}

The numerical Jacobian evaluated at the reported
second-stage estimates is
\[
    \widehat G_4
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -3.723984 & -0.652084\\
        -83.932987 & -14.706804
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The resulting parameter covariance matrix is
\[
    \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\widehat b_2)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        17239.161 & -98385.565\\
        -98385.565 & 561495.965
    \end{bmatrix},
\]
which implies standard errors
\[
    \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}(\widehat b_2)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        131.298\\
        749.330
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The AGS sensitivity matrix is
\[
    \widehat\Lambda_4
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        402.590 & -17.850\\
        -2297.615 & 101.942
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The singular values of the estimated Jacobian are
approximately $85.296$ and $0.000428$. The condition number
of $\widehat G_4^{\mathsf T}W_2\widehat G_4$ is approximately
$2.49\times10^{10}$.

These results indicate severe numerical ill-conditioning,
consistent withrank deficiency. The very
large standard errors and AGS sensitivities reflect the
difficulty of separately estimating the two parameters.
Since the Jacobian is rank deficient, the usual
asymptotic covariance and AGS formulas are not well-defined
at the true parameter values.

\paragraph{(d) J-test.}

The reported J-statistic is $J_{\mathrm{test}}=0.062679.$


\section*{Question 5}

We next consider the just-identified specification matching
the variance and first-order autocorrelation:
\[
    M_T(x)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        1.533191\\
        0.563408
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The population moments are
\[
    \mu_5(b)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \displaystyle\frac{\sigma^2}{1-\rho^2}\\[6pt]
        \rho
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The Jacobian at the true parameters is
\[
    G_5(b_0)
    =
    -
    \begin{bmatrix}
        16/9 & 8/3\\
        1 & 0
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

Since
\[
    \det(G_5(b_0))=-\frac{8}{3}\neq0,
\]
the Jacobian has full column rank. Unlike the specification
in Question 4, these moments locally identify both parameters.
In particular, autocorrelation identifies $\rho$, while
variance then identifies $\sigma$.

\paragraph{(a) First-stage estimation.}

Using $W_1=I_2$, we obtain

Figure \ref{fig:smm_surfaces}(b) displays the objective surface.
The parameter estimates are reasonably close to the true values

\paragraph{(b) Second-stage estimation.}

        0.576984\\

    \qquad

\paragraph{(c) Standard errors and AGS statistics.}

The numerical Jacobian is
\[
    \widehat G_5
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -2.610561 & -3.000709\\
        -0.985242 & 0.000000
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The estimated parameter covariance matrix is
\[
    \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\widehat b_2)

The first row indicates that the estimate of $\rho$ is

\section*{Question 6}

Finally, we consider the overidentified specification matching
the mean, variance, and first-order autocorrelation:
\[
    M_T(x)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.217749\\
        1.533191\\
        0.563408
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The corresponding population moment vector is
\[
    \mu_6(b)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0\\[4pt]
        \displaystyle\frac{\sigma^2}{1-\rho^2}\\[6pt]
        \rho
    \end{bmatrix},
\]
with Jacobian
\[
    G_6(b_0)
    =
    -
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & 0\\
        16/9 & 8/3\\
        1 & 0
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The Jacobian has full column rank, so the two parameters are
locally identified. Since we have three moments and two
parameters, the model is overidentified.

\paragraph{(a) First-stage estimation.}

Using the identity weighting matrix, we obtain
\[
    \widehat b_1
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.586628\\
        1.013961
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    J_{TH}(\widehat b_1)=0.035732.
\]

Figure \ref{fig:smm_surfaces}(c) presents the objective surface.
Unlike Question 5, the three moment conditions cannot in
general be matched exactly using only two parameters.

\paragraph{(b) Second-stage estimation.}

The estimated optimal weighting matrix is
\[
    W_2
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.227746 & 0.011538 & -0.005027\\
        0.011538 & 0.132807 & -0.217970\\
        -0.005027 & -0.217970 & 1.747638
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The second-stage estimates are
\[
    \widehat b_2
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.580953\\
        1.026909
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    J_{TH}(\widehat b_2)=0.008087.
\]

The optimal weighting matrix changes the relative importance
of the moment discrepancies, leading to slightly different
estimates from the first stage.

\paragraph{(c) Standard errors and AGS statistics.}

The numerical Jacobian is
\[
    \widehat G_6
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -0.073794 & -0.028155\\
        -2.691042 & -3.036088\\
        -0.985274 & 0.000000
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The resulting parameter covariance matrix is
\[
    \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\widehat b_2)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.003698 & -0.001317\\
        -0.001317 & 0.004546
    \end{bmatrix},
\]
and the standard errors are
\[
    \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}(\widehat b_2)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.060815\\
        0.067427
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The AGS sensitivity matrix is
\[
    \widehat\Lambda_6
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.020815 & -0.000193 & 1.013914\\
        0.026379 & 0.329127 & -0.900907
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The columns correspond to the mean, variance, and
autocorrelation, respectively.

The estimated sensitivities to the mean are relatively small.
This is consistent with the population mean being independent
of both structural parameters. The small nonzero sensitivities
arise from finite-sample variation and the estimated weighting
matrix.

As in Question 5, the estimate of $\rho$ is primarily sensitive
to autocorrelation, while $\sigma$ depends on both variance
and autocorrelation.

The Jacobian has singular values $4.1117$ and $0.7285$.
The condition number of
$\widehat G_6^{\mathsf T}W_2\widehat G_6$
is approximately $2.01$, indicating substantially better
numerical conditioning than in Question 4.

\paragraph{(d) J-test.}

The J-statistic is $J_{\mathrm{test}}=1.617454.$

\section*{Question 7}

We estimate the structural parameters of the AR(1) model using
indirect inference with an MA($N$) auxiliary model for
$N\in\{1,2,3\}$.

Recall that the stationary AR(1) process admits the
MA($\infty$) representation
\[
    x_t
    =
    \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\rho^j\varepsilon_{t-j}.
\]
We approximate this process using the auxiliary MA($N$) model
\[
    x_t
    =
    e_t+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\theta_j e_{t-j}.
\]

For each $N$, we estimate the auxiliary parameters
$\widehat\theta_N$ by minimizing the sum of squared
prediction errors. We additionally calculate the sample
standard deviation $\widehat s$ to provide information
about the scale parameter $\sigma$.

For any candidate structural parameter vector
$b=(\rho,\sigma)^{\mathsf T}$, we simulate $H=10$
AR(1) series and estimate the auxiliary MA($N$) model
on each series. We average the resulting auxiliary
estimates to obtain $\overline\theta_N(b)$ and
$\overline s(b)$.

We then minimize the indirect inference objective
\[
    Q_N(b)
    =
    \left(
        \overline\theta_N(b)-\widehat\theta_N
    \right)^{\mathsf T}
    \left(
        \overline\theta_N(b)-\widehat\theta_N
    \right)
    +
    \left(
        \overline s(b)-\widehat s
    \right)^2.
\]
The simulated innovations are held fixed across candidate
parameter values during optimization.

\paragraph{Results.}

The empirical auxiliary parameter estimates are
\[
    \widehat\theta_1
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.460187
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    \widehat\theta_2
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.508388\\
        0.360174
    \end{bmatrix},
\]
and
\[
    \widehat\theta_3
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0.581023\\
        0.421712\\
        0.160500
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]
The empirical sample standard deviation is
$\widehat s=1.241328$.

Table \ref{tab:indirect_inference} reports the estimated
structural parameters for each auxiliary specification.
